# A 365-Day Chronological Bible Reading Plan

*Draft 4: with supplementary readings covering the whole Bible.*

## How the plan is built

The plan reads Scripture in the order events happened (or, for prophets and letters, when they were most likely written), tracing one covenant of grace from creation to new creation and its fulfillment in Christ (Luke 24:27; WCF 7.5–6). Each group notes where it places a book whose date is uncertain.

**Plan days** are numbered 1–365. Narrative days stay at three chapters or fewer, every epistle day is exactly one chapter, and psalms marked with "+" are short add-ons tied to the event.

**Supplementary readings** are marked **S** with the passage in italics. Together with the plan, they cover every chapter of the Bible. Each one sits at its best-guess chronological place and groups together consecutive chapters of one book that the plan skips at that point, so some are long (Leviticus 17–27, Psalms 58–71). Where a supplement belongs between two chapters read on the same plan day, it is listed right after that day and its note says so. In the Gospels, supplements are the parallel accounts, listed right after the day that reads the same events. They are optional and untimed: read them alongside the plan, save them for a second year, or skip them.

WCF (Westminster Confession of Faith) and WSC (Westminster Shorter Catechism) notes point to readings that anchor a doctrine in the Westminster Standards. Every section of the Confession has at least one of its 1647 proof texts in the plan days.

## Group 2: From Egypt to the Promised Land: Moses, Joshua, Judges, Ruth (Days 26–61)

Redemption from Egypt (c. 1446 BC on the early-date view common among conservative Reformed scholars), Sinai, the wilderness, and the conquest. Law sections are kept to the Ten Commandments, plus Leviticus 16 because Hebrews 9 builds on it. Judges 17–21 is read near the start of the judges period because Phinehas is still serving (Judg 20:28); Ruth is placed mid-period, after Gideon.
